How the numbers work

Sections: the cable is split into lengths with different weight in seawater — for example bare cable and cable with CPS fitted. The float type stays the same; each section gets its own spacing, number of tails per float and tail spec.

Load per float = SF × section weight in seawater × section spacing. It must not exceed the float's net buoyancy (usable lift after the float's own weight, rigging and any derating).

Max spacing = net buoyancy ÷ (SF × weight). The suggested spacing is rounded down to a practical step.

One-float-loss check: if a float fails, the floats either side each take half of its span, so they carry weight × 1.5 × spacing (no SF). Above 100% there is a risk of chain failure: the next floats go under one after another.

Float count: each section is floated from its start at its own spacing, with a float at both ends of the floated length. Spares % is added to floats and tails.

SF below 1 is a deliberate under-float: the cable will sag below the surface between floats.
